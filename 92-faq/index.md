## What is this book?

This book is a near real-time, distilled record of what I'm learning about how to structure a software factory.

Putting my insights into book form forces me to organize them into a coherent mental model, and hopefully makes them useful to others, too. And of course, as I go, all of this will be easy for some future AI to take in and build on!

It's my hope that anyone who sees an opportunity to improve this book will do so. Ultimately, I'd like this to become a community-authored work.

## How does this book get updated?

Whenever I learn something about AI-driven software development, or about how to build a software factory around it, I record it in [fabricahq/book](https://github.com/fabricahq/book).

These insights come often, and they don't arrive in neat chunks, so I skip pull requests and commit straight to `main`.

To keep that history readable, I group commits into _updates_. An update is one meaningful change to the book. It might be a new section, or a rewrite of an old one. Every commit message ends with a one-line note naming its update, such as `Update: hierarchy-of-bottlenecks`. Git hooks enforce these notes.

The book's [changelog](/book/changes/) lists updates rather than commits, so you see one entry per meaningful change instead of every small edit. To see every commit to a page, click the "Updated" date under its title.

## How do you know that everything in this book is accurate?

Everything here comes from building and running my own software factory, or from people whose ideas I credit by name, and I revise it as I learn more.

Of course, software factories are evolving literally by the day, and like everyone else, I'm figuring it out as I go. So think of this book as a work in progress that reflects my best thinking so far. In time, I hope it converges on established industry best practices.

Naturally, I'd welcome your feedback! [Open an issue](https://github.com/fabricahq/book/issues/new/choose) or [send a pull request](https://github.com/fabricahq/book/pulls) in the book's repository. Every page also ends with a "Suggest an edit to this page" link that opens an issue for that page.

## What do Planned, Draft, and Written mean?

Every page has a status, and the contents shows each one:

- **Planned:** not written yet. The page may have an outline or some rough notes, but I haven't written or reviewed it.
- **Draft:** written, but it may not cover everything I have to say on the topic, and I haven't given it a final edit.
- **Written:** finished, at least for now.

## How can I follow along?

Check the book's [changelog](/book/changes/), which lists every meaningful update. To see every change as it lands, watch [fabricahq/book](https://github.com/fabricahq/book) on GitHub.
