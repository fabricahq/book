Here's what a good afternoon with agents looks like.

You kick off a feature. While an agent builds it, you kick off a second piece of work, maybe a bug fix in another part of the code. When the feature reaches a point worth looking at, you review it, give your feedback, and send it back to work. Then you check on the bug fix. Then you kick off the next thing.

That's orchestration. Many things happen at once, but you only ever do one of them: the review in front of you. So the real skill is choosing when to look. Look too often, and you pay switching costs all day. Look too rarely, and an agent spends an hour building the wrong thing.

I call these moments _review checkpoints_: points you plan ahead of time where you review an agent's work, give feedback, and launch the next piece of work. This chapter is about setting them up well. It starts with whether you [need orchestration at all](01-do-you-need-it.md), then covers why [your attention is the bottleneck](02-human-attention.md), the [patterns](03-patterns.md) that let agents run in parallel between checkpoints, and how to [choose which workflows to adopt](04-choosing-workflows.md).
